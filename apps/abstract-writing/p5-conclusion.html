5: Conclusion

← Back to Menu

Running example: predicting adverse events in hyperkalemia

The conclusion is 1 to 2 sentences. State the main finding succinctly, then discuss secondary outcomes or next steps. Every claim should point straight back to a result reported above. No new numbers, and nothing the results cannot support.

    The running example

    Tap each conclusion sentence to light up the results it rests on.

    Common mistakes

    • Making claims that are too broad based on the study design.
    • Editorializing on the extrapolation of the results beyond the study population.
    • Ending on a vague “more research is needed.” If you name next steps, make them specific.
